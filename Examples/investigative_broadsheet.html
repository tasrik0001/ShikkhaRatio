The Missing Educators: When One Must Bear the Burden of Eighty.

Despite international guidelines capping classroom sizes, national data reveals a systemic fracture in structural teaching capacity.

By The Ledger Data Desk Data Sourced: Open Ledger

A

t the core of the UNESCO educational framework lies a simple metric: 1 to 30. One trained educator for every thirty students. It is a ratio designed to guarantee baseline attention, adequate cognitive development, and classroom stability.

Yet, analysis of the latest anonymous public ledger submissions paints a drastically divergent reality for primary education zones across the nation. The national average currently sits at a precarious 1:47.

"We are no longer teaching; we are managing crowds," a submission from a primary school in Kurigram notes. In regions like Sunamganj and Bhola, the data shows an even sharper deviation, with single teachers frequently anchored to classrooms exceeding eighty students.

Critical Deviation Zones

  • Kurigram 1:85
  • Sunamganj 1:82
  • Bhola 1:78

The interactive visualization provided here illustrates the cognitive and structural strain of these escalating ratios. As the slider scales past the recommended threshold, the resulting network mapping demonstrates the impossibility of individualized educational attention.
