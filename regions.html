Beyond the national figure / Dataset year 2024

A national average cannot tell every district’s story.

Bangladesh’s districts share an education system, but not the same balance of students and teachers. Looking at those differences brings the staffing question closer to home.

The distance between districts

Secondary-school ratios range from 20.72 students per teacher in Jessore to 49.91 in Sunamganj. That is more than twice as many reported students per teacher at the upper end. The national figure of 30.90 does not show that spread on its own.

Colleges show a wider range: 17.47 in Natore to 77.71 in Bandarban, with a national ratio of 37.10. These comparisons describe the balance of reported students and teachers, not the number of students sitting in a particular classroom.

What does the middle look like?

Line up the 64 districts from lowest to highest ratio and the median marks the middle. It is 31.47 for secondary schools and 38.71 for colleges. This gives another way to understand the distribution without focusing only on its extremes.

The district mean gives every district equal weight. The national ratio instead divides all students by all teachers, so districts with more teachers contribute more to it. They answer different questions, which is why the two numbers differ.

Why might places differ?

Institution sizes, programmes, enrolment and the distribution of teachers are all worth looking at. Travel and subject needs may also matter. The district figures do not tell us which of these explains a particular difference.

Even a district near the national average can contain institutions with very different needs. Asking whether staffing is fair means looking inside the district as well as comparing it with its neighbours.

Make a comparison that means something

In the data explorer, choose a sector and compare districts within it. Read the student, teacher and institution counts alongside the ratio. Schools and colleges cover different settings, so keeping them separate makes comparisons clearer.
