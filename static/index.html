The Shikkha Deficit / Bangladesh, 2024

Every student deserves a teacher's time.

Education depends on the time teachers can give. How is that time shared across Bangladesh?

By Nirob and Rehan · Education figures: BANBEIS 2024

Education or crowd control?

When I visited my mother's primary school, I sometimes saw her teaching around 50 children. When another teacher was absent, she sometimes had to look after two classes at once. To me, it looked more like crowd control than a lesson. She would come home exhausted.

That memory is why ShikkhaRatio exists. Behind every staffing discussion are children waiting for help and teachers trying to give it. Read the story behind this project.

One teacher, different numbers of students

The 2024 secondary-school figures cover 9,063,422 students and 293,289 teachers. Dividing students by teachers gives 30.90 students per teacher across the country. This measure helps us compare how many students there are relative to the teaching staff.

Look beyond the countrywide figure and the differences become clearer. Sunamganj has 49.91 students per teacher, while Jessore has 20.72. These are district-wide ratios: they describe the students and teachers across many schools together.

What would it take for every child to get the help they need? The answer begins with understanding the people and schools behind these numbers.
