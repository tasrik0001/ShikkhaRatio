The score asks how evenly the district ratios sit side by side. A score of 100 would mean every district has the same ratio, so a lower score means wider gaps between places.

How this number is worked out

We take the 64 district ratios, find how far each one sits from the middle value, and divide that spread by the middle value. That ratio is called the coefficient of variation: a small number means the districts sit close together, a large number means they are spread wide. The score is 100 minus that number, so 100 would mean every district matches exactly and a lower score means bigger gaps. The three bands are a reading guide for this page, not an official scale.

How this number is worked out

Correlation, written r, runs from -1 to 1. Near 0 means no steady relationship, a positive value means the two tend to rise together, and a negative value means one tends to fall as the other rises. We calculate it across all 64 districts for students and the ratio, institutions and the ratio, and the share of women teachers and the ratio. Correlation only says whether two patterns travel together, it never proves that one causes the other.

How this number is worked out

Variance is a way of measuring how spread out the district ratios are around their average. We split that spread into two parts: the gaps between the eight division averages, and the gaps between districts sitting inside the same division. Each division counts in proportion to its number of districts, so divisions with more districts pull harder on the total. The two shares always add up to 100 percent.

How this number is worked out

This scenario uses the secondary-school figures, where the national average is 30.90 students per teacher. For every district above 35 students per teacher we count the teachers it would take to bring it down to 35, rounding up so the cap really holds. For every district below the national average we count the teachers it could release while still sitting at 30.90, rounding down so that floor really holds. The net compares the two counts, and this is a bookkeeping exercise rather than a staffing plan.
