A student’s account / By Tasrik

It started in my mother’s classroom.

Before I started looking at district numbers, I watched my mom try to teach a room full of children. I did not understand the problem then the way I do now.

What I remember

I’m Tasrik, a class 8 student. My mom works as a teacher in a primary school near my village. Before class 5, when I was growing up in the village, I visited her school many times.

Sometimes I saw her taking a class with around 50 students. Sometimes, because other teachers were absent, she had to take two classes at the same time, around 100 children altogether. Those are the approximate numbers I remember from my visits.

To me, that felt less like a class and more like crowd control. How could one person give all those children attention at once? My mom would come home exhausted and irritated. At the time, I saw it without really understanding what it meant.

The question stayed with me

As I got older, I started understanding things I had not understood even a few months earlier. I thought again about those visits. If a teacher spends so much energy just managing a room, what is left for teaching?

That is the question behind ShikkhaRatio. I wanted to look beyond what I had seen in one school and make the numbers easier to understand. A spreadsheet can feel far away from a classroom. I wanted a way to begin with a place people know and ask what its figures might mean.

From one classroom to a wider question

The data here covers secondary schools and colleges in 2024, rather than primary schools like my mom’s. It gives me another way to think about students and teachers across Bangladesh, while my memories explain why I wanted to ask in the first place.

A district ratio cannot tell me what every teacher’s day is like. But it can help people notice differences, look at the counts behind them, and ask more about the schools and colleges around them.

What I hope people take away

I want people to look at their district and think about the people behind the numbers. I hope this project can become useful to people working to improve education. For now, making the question easier to see and discuss is a place to begin.

You can start with your district, read about teacher availability, or see what I hope to build next.
